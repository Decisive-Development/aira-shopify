Study 01Physiology

Your sinuses make nitric oxide.

Breathing through the nose produces nitric oxide in the paranasal sinuses, a molecule that helps widen blood vessels and supports oxygen uptake. Mouth breathing skips it entirely.

Breathing routeSinus nitric oxide
Through the noseCarried into the airway
Through the mouthBypassed entirely

Table 1  Nitric oxide delivery by breathing route.

Lundberg, J.O.N., et al. (1995). High nitric oxide production in human paranasal sinuses. Acta Physiologica Scandinavica, 154(4), 431-434.

Study 02Epidemiology

Blocked noses make for tired days.

Adults with night-time nasal obstruction were 2.4x more likely to report being tired all day than those breathing freely.

Fig. 1  Relative odds of daytime sleepiness, schematic.

Young, T., Finn, L., Kim, H. (1997). Nasal obstruction as a risk factor for sleep-disordered breathing. Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology, 99(2), S757-S762.

Study 03Rhinology

The people who really judge snoring: partners.

Over a 14-night trial of nasal strips, partners rated the snoring quieter. Not a lab score: the verdict of the person lying next to it.

Fig. 2  Partner-rated snoring intensity across the trial, schematic.

Ulfberg, J., Fenton, G. (1997). Effect of Breathe Right nasal strip on snoring. Rhinology, 35(2), 50-52.

Study 04Otolaryngology

It works even when the septum doesn't.

Even with a deviated septum, opening the nasal valve from the outside increases airflow. The valve, not the septum, is the gatekeeper.

Fig. 3  Airflow with external dilation, deviated-septum subjects, schematic.

Roithmann, R., Chapnik, J., Cole, P., Szalai, J., Zamel, N. (1998). Role of the external nasal dilator in the management of nasal obstruction. Laryngoscope, 108(5), 712-715.

Study 05Sleep medicine, RCT

The gold standard: a randomised trial.

In a four-week randomised controlled trial, nasal dilation significantly improved sleep quality and daytime function in people with chronic sleep-maintenance insomnia.

Fig. 4  Sleep quality across the four-week trial, schematic.

Krakow, B., Melendrez, D., Sisley, B., et al. (2006). Nasal dilator strip therapy for chronic sleep-maintenance insomnia and symptoms of sleep-disordered breathing: a randomized controlled trial. Sleep and Breathing, 10(1), 16-28.

Study 06Sports science

Trained noses run more efficiently.

Recreational runners moved 22% less air for the same pace when breathing through the nose, the same work on far less ventilation.

Fig. 5  Minute ventilation at a fixed pace, schematic.

Dallam, G.M., McClaran, S.R., Cox, D.G., Foust, C.P. (2018). Effect of nasal versus oral breathing on VO2max and physiological economy in recreational runners. International Journal of Kinesiology and Sports Science, 6(2), 22-29.

Study 07Epidemiology, UAE

This is not a faraway problem.

In a UAE study, 1 in 3 adults snored, and nearly half of snorers were at high risk for sleep apnoea. The nights this research describes are happening here.

Fig. 6  Self-reported snoring and apnoea risk, UAE adults, schematic.

Al Shaikh, Y.G., et al. (2018). The symptoms and risk of sleep apnea among adults in the United Arab Emirates. Annals of Thoracic Medicine, 13(3), 168-174.
